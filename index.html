Our Philosophy

Crafted with patience,
served with purpose

Velour was born from a single conviction — that a great meal is never just food. It is atmosphere, conversation, memory. Nestled in the heart of Clifton, our dining room is a quiet rebellion against the ordinary.

Every plate that leaves our kitchen carries the labour of chefs who trained across Paris, Tokyo, and Istanbul — returning home to Karachi with techniques that honour the world without abandoning its roots.

Our Story

"To cook is to give — to dine at Velour is to receive without condition."

Velour opened in 2012 in a narrow townhouse on Sunset Boulevard. There were twelve seats, one oven, and a head chef who had spent seven years in Lyon. The reservation book was full within a week.

Twelve years later we occupy the entire building. The oven has multiplied. But the conviction remains unchanged: forty guests, one sitting, nothing between the kitchen and you but intention.

2012
A narrow door opens
Chef Reza Malik returns from Lyon and opens Velour in a twelve-seat townhouse. The menu is handwritten each morning. The reservation book fills before the paint dries. Word travels faster than any advertisement.
2015
The cellar is born
Sommelier Sara Naqvi joins the team, transforming a basement storeroom into a 400-label wine cellar. The first formal pairing menu launches — four courses, four pours. The table is no longer just about food.
2018
Expansion with restraint
Velour acquires the adjacent property, growing to forty seats — but refusing to add a second sitting. "We would rather turn people away than rush them," Chef Malik tells Dawn. Three private dining rooms open.
2021
The Chef's Table arrives
Six seats are placed at the pass. The nightly tasting menu debuts. In its first year, the Chef's Table is booked six weeks in advance on average. It is now the most requested experience in the house.
2024
Twelve years of intention
Velour turns twelve. The cellar holds 800 labels. The team numbers 34. The menu is still written by hand. A second location is proposed, discussed, and quietly declined. One restaurant, done properly, is enough.

What we believe

Four commitments

I
Restraint over excess
Forty seats is not a limitation. It is a decision. We would rather do one thing extraordinarily than many things adequately.
II
Seasonality above all
The menu bends to the market, not the other way around. When mangoes peak, the kitchen stops and listens. When they're gone, we move on.
III
The guest is always present
We remember your preferences. We notice when you're celebrating. The dining room is yours for the evening; we are simply its caretakers.
IV
No compromise on craft
The stock takes 48 hours. The pasta is rolled each afternoon. The bread is baked twice. These things take time. That is the point.
